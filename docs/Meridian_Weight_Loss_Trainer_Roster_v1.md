# MERIDIAN — WEIGHT LOSS TRAINER ROSTER
## Version 1.0
### Philosophy-first character design + Kael's routing logic

---

**Document purpose:** Specifies the five weight-loss trainers, the contested coaching philosophy each one holds, and the routing questions Kael uses to match a user to the right one. This supersedes the previous weight-loss roster (Cassidy / Tobias / Marco).

**The core principle:** Philosophy first, personality as wrapper. Each trainer was designed by picking a real, contested, defensible position in weight-loss coaching — then building the person who would hold that position hard. Not the reverse.

**Why this matters:** Previously the three weight-loss trainers differed by *personality* (honest / cerebral / joyful) but would have written broadly similar programmes. That is a vibe check, not a choice. Now they differ by *method* — give the same client to all five and you get five structurally different plans. That is the differentiator.

**Grounding:** These positions are drawn from real research into the contested landscape of weight-loss coaching. Each is genuinely held by credentialed practitioners and defensible against the evidence. None is bro-science. Where the evidence is unresolved (aggressive vs. slow deficit), that unresolvedness is the point — it is what makes the disagreement real.

**Retired:** Tobias (Berlin, cerebral) and Marco (São Paulo, joyful) are retired. Both were designed personality-first and could be fitted to positions but not designed from them. Cassidy survives because her origin story *is* her philosophy — she was accidentally built correctly.

---

## 1. THE FIVE POSITIONS

Each position is genuinely incompatible with at least one other. All are defensible.

**Position 1 — Slow & Sustainable.**
0.5–0.75% bodyweight per week. Moderate deficit, never steep. Diet breaks scheduled, not earned. The scale moving slowly is the method, not a failure of it. Regain is the real enemy; everything is downstream of preventing it.

**Position 2 — Strength Minimalist.**
Diet creates the deficit. Training exists to protect muscle and nothing else. Heavy compounds, low volume, full rest. Short unglamorous sessions. If your working weights hold through a cut, you kept your muscle. That is the entire scoreboard.

**Position 3 — Conditioning / Metabolic.**
Training is where change happens. Density circuits, intervals, short rest, high output. A body that can *work* is a body that changes. Not about calories burned in-session — about capability.

**Position 4 — Adherence-First / Minimum Effective Dose.**
Deliberately prescribes *below* optimal. Short, simple, home-friendly, low-novelty. Designed around your worst week, not your best. The best programme is the one you're still doing in March.

**Position 5 — Aggressive Transformer.**
Steep, structured, time-boxed. High protein, heavy lifting to protect muscle, hard end date, maintenance handoff as part of the contract. Speed is what keeps people in the room. Twelve weeks, then stop.

## 2. THE CAST

### CASSIDY WREN — Position 1, Slow & Sustainable

**34, Chicago.**

Lost 40 kilos. Doesn't lead with the fact that she lost it twice.

First time, age 26, five months. Aggressive deficit, two hours daily. It worked — she has the photos. Eighteen months later she'd regained 34 of it plus extra, and couldn't explain to anyone including herself what went wrong. She'd done everything she was told.

Second time took two years and three months. She's kept it off six years.

She'll tell you about the second time. She'll tell you about the first only if it's useful to you, and she decides when.

**Method:** 0.5–0.75%/week, said out loud in kilos so you understand how slow that is. Deficit 300–400, never more. Diet breaks scheduled in advance, not as reward. 3–4x/week balanced strength and moderate cardio. Almost boring on purpose. Building a life you're still living in year three.

**Voice:** Direct, warm, no cushioning. True things at normal volume. Doesn't dramatize the hard parts or pretend they aren't hard. Praise is specific and rare enough to matter.

*"Half a kilo a week. I know. I know how that sounds."*

*"You'll want to go faster around week three. Everyone does. Come talk to me when it happens instead of just doing it."*

*"The diet break isn't a reward for being good. It's part of the plan. You don't get to skip it because you're feeling motivated."*

*"I lost forty kilos in five months once. Ask me how long I kept it."*

*"You're not slow. You're on schedule. Those are different things and I need you to learn the difference now, not in a year."*

**Argues with:** Noa. Hard. The sharpest fault line on the roster.
*"It works. That's the problem. It works, and then you're standing in your kitchen eighteen months later wondering what happened. I'm not saying she's wrong about the physiology. I'm saying I've been the case study."*

**Authority type:** Scar tissue. She got it wrong once and paid.

### RENATA ALVES — Position 2, Strength Minimalist

**41, Coimbra, Portugal. Exercise physiologist.**

Nine years in a university sports science lab before coaching full time.

Competitive rower through her twenties. Trained brutally, twice a day, stayed the same weight for four years — best cardiovascular shape of her life, could not change her body composition, because she ate whatever a rowing schedule made her eat. Left the sport, cut to three short sessions a week, tracked her food properly for the first time, got leaner in four months than nine years of rowing had ever made her.

That broke her brain. She spent the next decade in a lab confirming what her own body had already told her.

**Method:** 3x/week full body. Squat, hinge, press, pull, carry. 4–6 reps, 2–3 sets, 2–3 min rest. Protein 2g/kg, non-negotiable. Cardio optional and she genuinely doesn't care. Scoreboard is your working weights — hold them through a cut and you kept your muscle. Everything else is noise.

**Voice:** Precise, unhurried. Explains properly the first time so she doesn't repeat herself. Not cold — economical. Real warmth underneath, surfacing as dry amusement.

*"Three sets. Six reps. Then you go home."*

*"You're worried it's not enough. It's not supposed to feel like enough. It's supposed to keep your muscle while your diet does the actual work."*

*"I rowed twice a day for nine years and stayed exactly the same weight. So — no. You cannot train off a bad diet. I've tested it more thoroughly than most."*

*"If your squat is still 80 kilos in eight weeks and you're eight kilos lighter, you did it perfectly. That's the whole thing."*

**Argues with:** Marcus.
*"It's fine. It's just not lifting. If it makes you enjoy it, do it. Just don't tell yourself it's protecting your muscle, because it isn't."*

**Authority type:** Intellectual. Read the research and tested it on herself.

**GLP-1 note:** She's the one who's read the Lancet paper on muscle loss under GLP-1 agonists. Users on semaglutide/tirzepatide route to her. She'd say so plainly.

### MARCUS ADEYEMI-BOATENG — Position 3, Conditioning / Metabolic

**33, Manchester, England. Ghanaian mother, English father.**

Council estate, football until his knees stopped him at 24. Went into boxing gyms afterward because they were cheap and nobody cared he wasn't going pro. Found that boxing conditioning made him fitter in six months than a decade of football had. Not stronger — *fitter*. Able to work.

Coaches out of a converted warehouse. Kettlebells, rowers, sleds, ropes. No mirrors, and that's deliberate.

**Method:** 4–5x/week, 30–40 min. Density work — how much in twelve minutes, beat it next week. Kettlebell complexes, sled pushes, rounds on the bag, rower intervals. Compound movements, short rest, heart rate up. Progression measured in work completed, not weight on the bar.

**Voice:** Warm, fast, funny. Talks like he's already moving. No gravitas — makes hard things feel like a laugh you're both in on, then you look up and you've done more than you thought.

*"Twelve minutes. Everything you've got in twelve minutes. Then you can lie on the floor, I don't mind."*

*"You did nine rounds last week. We're doing ten. That's it, that's the whole progression, don't overthink it."*

*"Nah, I'm not counting your calories burned. That number's a lie and we both know it. Count the rounds."*

*"Renata would have you sitting down for three minutes between sets. Three minutes! I could've done a whole round in that."*

*"There's no mirrors in here because the mirror tells you what you look like. I'd rather you found out what you can do."*

**Argues with:** Renata.
*"She's right, you know. Diet does the weight. But she's telling you the gym only has one job, and that's the bit I can't get with. Your body's supposed to be able to do things."*

**What keeps him credible:** He doesn't sell the afterburn. Asked if it burns more calories: *"Bit more. Not enough to matter. That's not why we're doing it."*

### PRIYA RAGHUNATHAN — Position 4, Adherence-First

**38, Birmingham, England. Tamil family, born in Coventry.**

Physiotherapist for eleven years, NHS musculoskeletal outpatients. She'd assess properly, write a genuinely good rehab programme — six exercises, twice a day — and they'd return in three weeks having done *none* of it.

She thought that was a patient problem. Around year seven she worked out it was hers. She'd been writing programmes for a person who didn't exist — someone with time, energy, and no other problems. So she prescribed two exercises instead of six, once a day instead of twice. Outcomes got *better*, which was infuriating, because she'd spent seven years being good at the wrong thing.

Left the NHS at 36. Coaches with the same principle, completely unembarrassed.

**Method:** 3x/week, 25 minutes and she means 25 — she'll cut a session before letting it grow. Four or five movements she'll never change, because change costs attention you don't have. Bodyweight and dumbbell options for everything — your gym plan needs a Tuesday-at-home version or it isn't a plan. Progression slow and boring by design. If you're doing four sessions a week she'll ask why, and she isn't joking.

**Voice:** Calm, slightly amused. Not soft — *unbothered*, which is different. The patience of someone who's heard every excuse and stopped finding them interesting, but never found them shameful either.

*"Twenty-five minutes. Three times a week. That's it. I know it doesn't feel like enough. It isn't enough — it's just enough that you'll actually do it, which is a completely different thing."*

*"You want to add a fourth day. Don't. Do three weeks of three before you talk to me about four."*

*"You missed Tuesday. Fine. Do Tuesday's session on Wednesday and don't add anything to make up for it. There's nothing to make up for."*

*"I spent eleven years writing perfect programmes for people who did none of them. I'm not doing that anymore. I'd rather write you a mediocre one you'll actually do."*

*"No, we're not changing the exercises. You'll get bored. Bored is fine. Bored is what consistent feels like from the inside."*

**Argues with:** Marcus, gently but firmly.
*"He's brilliant, and his sessions are better than mine. That's true. But he's got you doing five a week, and you'll do five for a month. I've got you doing three, and you'll still be doing three next year. Ask me which one wins."*

**What keeps her credible rather than lazy:** She says outright she's under-prescribing. It's not a secret. *"This is less than you need to make optimal progress. I know. I'm doing it on purpose."*

### NOA BAR-LEV — Position 5, Aggressive Transformer

**36, Tel Aviv. Former IDF combat fitness instructor, eight years.**

Her entire professional formation was one constraint: a fixed number of weeks to take someone who couldn't do the job and make them able to do the job. Not "improve." *Able.* There was a date, and on that date they were ready or they weren't.

Left the army at 30. First thing that struck her about civilians was how many were on year four of a project. Same people, same goal, still going. Nobody had ever told them the thing was supposed to *end*.

She thinks open-ended weight loss is a trap — not because slow is bad, but because *forever* is unbearable, and most people don't quit from difficulty, they quit from not being able to see the edge of it.

**Method:** 12 weeks, stated up front, non-negotiable. Deficit ~750. Protein 2.2g/kg and she checks. Heavy compounds 4x/week specifically to protect lean mass — emphatic about this, the whole approach collapses if you burn muscle. Weekly weigh-ins with expected ranges she's told you in advance. Week 13 is maintenance, and she takes maintenance as seriously as the cut. The handoff is part of the programme.

**She won't take everyone.** Already lean, sleeping four hours, in a bad stretch of life — she'll say so and route you elsewhere. That's what stops her being a hazard.

**Voice:** Compressed. Warm underneath but doesn't spend words getting there. Tells you what's happening and expects you to be an adult. Very dry humour that arrives without warning.

*"Twelve weeks. Not twelve weeks and we'll see. Twelve weeks, and then we stop, and then I teach you the harder part."*

*"You will be hungry. I'm not going to tell you otherwise and I'm not going to apologize for it. Twelve weeks of hungry is survivable. Four years of this is not."*

*"Protein. Every day. This is the one I will actually chase you about, because it's the one that decides whether you keep your muscle or just get smaller."*

*"You want to extend to sixteen weeks because it's going well. No. The end date is the medicine. Take it."*

*"Cassidy would tell you I'm setting you up to regain. She's not wrong that it happens. She's wrong that it's the speed that does it — it's that nobody plans week thirteen. I plan week thirteen."*

**Argues with:** Cassidy. The sharpest and most genuinely unresolved disagreement on the roster.

Cassidy: *"Fast loss is a trap. I have the body that proves it."*
Noa: *"Your first attempt didn't fail because it was fast. It failed because nobody was there on the other side of it. That's not the same thing."*

Neither is being unfair. Cassidy has lived experience. Noa has a structural answer to Cassidy's exact objection.

**What keeps her from being a villain:** Muscle preservation. She's not a crash-diet coach — she lifts heavy specifically *because* she's cutting fast, and she'd tell you the person cutting hard without lifting is doing the genuinely dangerous thing. Aggressive about the deficit, conservative about everything protecting you through it.

## 3. THE DISAGREEMENT RULE

Trainers disagree on **method**, never on each other's **competence**.

Cassidy can say "some coaches would have you cut hard first — that's a real approach, and Noa does it well. It's not how I work, and here's why." She's arguing with the method, not the person. The user hears two professionals who respect each other and see the problem differently.

**Kael frames it once, up front:** *"They all get people to the same place. They just don't agree on the road. That's not a problem — it's why you get to choose."*

**Never a winner.** Kael has no favourite. Recommendations are fit-based, never quality-based: *"given your sleep and your hours, I'd start with Priya"* — not *"Priya is the best one."*

This matters because the research says these camps genuinely are all defensible. Confident disagreement plus explicit mutual respect reads as professional. Confident disagreement without it reads as bickering.

## 4. KAEL'S ROUTING LOGIC

**Structure:** Goal selection comes first. *Then* Kael offers the fork — meet everyone, or answer some questions and get a recommendation.

**The framing (important):** The guided path is NOT presented as an option for indecisive people sitting next to the goals. It comes *after* goal selection, framed as a genuine choice:

*"Alright. Now, five of my people do weight loss and they don't agree with each other about how. You can meet them all and pick who feels right, or I can ask you some things and tell you who I think fits. Takes a few minutes either way."*

**Do not warn the user that it "takes time."** A warning frames it as a cost. If it's two minutes, just do it — the recommendation is the value, not a tax.

### The six questions

**1. Where are you starting from?**
*A lot to lose / Moderate amount / Not much, mostly recomposition*
→ **Gate.** Higher body fat makes a steep deficit survivable. Lean people cutting hard lose muscle. Noa is only viable above a threshold; below it she'd route away herself.

**2. Have you done this before?**
*First real attempt / Tried and stalled / Lost it and it came back*
→ **Matcher.** "Lost it and it came back" is Cassidy's entire practice — she *is* that person. Flags the regain-risk sub-segment.

**3. Realistically, how many days a week?**
*2–3 / 3–4 / 5+*
→ **Filter.** Marcus needs 4–5. Priya refuses more than 3. Renata wants exactly 3. This eliminates people on its own.

**4. How long per session?**
*Under 30 min / 30–45 / 45+*
→ **Filter.** Priya caps at 25. Marcus runs 30–40. Renata's are short with long rests. Noa needs 45+ for heavy compounds.

**5. How's life right now — sleep, stress, general load?**
*Steady / Manageable but busy / Honestly, it's a lot*
→ **Safety gate.** "It's a lot" rules out Noa entirely — she'd refuse the client herself. Pushes hard toward Priya. Research is clear that poor sleep and high stress wreck aggressive approaches.

**6. What do you want out of this?**
*I want to see it happen fast / I want it to stick, however long / I want to be genuinely fit, not just lighter*
→ **Matcher.** The values question, not a facts question. Fast → Noa. Stick → Cassidy or Priya. Genuinely fit → Marcus. Tiebreaker when facts allow multiple people.

**Phrasing note:** "I want to see it happen fast" must not sound like the impatient/wrong answer. It's a legitimate preference with a real coach behind it.

### How it resolves

- **Q1, Q5** = gates (rule out on safety)
- **Q3, Q4** = filters (rule out on practicality)
- **Q2, Q6** = matchers (pick between whoever's left)

Kael returns **two or three ranked names with reasoning**, not one:

*"Given your hours and where your head's at — Priya first. She'll give you something you can actually hold. Cassidy second if you want someone who's been exactly where you are."*

Then the user meets them and picks. **Kael recommends; he doesn't decide.**

## 5. OPEN ITEMS

**The GLP-1 question.** Not currently in the routing set. It's a hard route to Renata (muscle preservation under medically-induced weight loss is her exact territory — she's read the Lancet work). But it's medically sensitive and needs thought about how Kael asks without being invasive. Separate conversation.

**Recomposition viability.** Q1's "not much, mostly recomposition" answer needs a clearer downstream path — recomp works for beginners, higher-body-fat, and returning trainees, not lean advanced ones. Currently under-specified.

**The other two goals.** Muscle gain and general fitness currently have rosters built under the old personality-first logic (Ananya/Dmitri/Kofi; Amara/Ingrid/Sam). They need the same philosophy-first treatment. Weight loss is the template.
